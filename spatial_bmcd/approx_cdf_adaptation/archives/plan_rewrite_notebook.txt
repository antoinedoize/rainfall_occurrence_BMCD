## The method, stated precisely (drives the explanation cells)

The hot spot of the pairwise composite likelihood is the standardized bivariate
normal CDF `q_joint = Φ₂(z1, z2; ρ)`, computed today by
[`phi2`](spatial_bmcd/spatial_model.py#L405-L409) (a fresh
`scipy.stats.multivariate_normal` per call) inside the scalar `for j,k` loop of
[`pairwise_loglik_one_step_nanaware`](spatial_bmcd/spatial_model.py#L412-L460).

The method rests on the exact 1-D integral identity

```
Φ₂(x1, x2; ρ) = ∫_{-∞}^{x2} φ(s) · Φ(a·s + b) ds,   a = -ρ/√(1-ρ²),  b = x1/√(1-ρ²)
```

which is exactly what the code's `a`, `b`, and `aq = a·x2 + b` encode (`aq` is
the inner-Φ argument at the upper limit). **Tsay–Ke's one trick:** replace the
inner univariate Φ, on each side of its zero, by a `½·exp(quadratic)`
tail-shaped function with **two fitted constants** `c1 = -1.0950081470333`,
`c2 = -0.75651138383854`. Then `φ(s)·(approx)` is `exp(quadratic in s)`, so the
integral collapses to a **closed-form `erf` expression**. The **4 sign branches**
are pure bookkeeping: `a > 0 / a ≤ 0` orients the line, and the split at
`s* = -b/a` (sign of `aq`) selects which side's tail form applies. Cost: a
branch-free `erf` formula, error ≈10⁻³ vs exact, and — being closed-form in ρ —
it **vectorises over per-pair ρ**.
